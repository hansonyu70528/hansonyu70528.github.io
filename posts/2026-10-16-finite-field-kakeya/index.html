A Kakeya set in \(\mathbb{F}_q^n\) is a set \(K\) that contains a line in every direction: for every \(v \in \mathbb{F}_q^n \setminus \left\{0\right\}\) there is a point \(x\) with \(\left\{x + tv : t \in \mathbb{F}_q\right\} \subseteq K\). Wolff asked whether every such set has size at least \(c_n q^n\),1 and Dvir answered the question with a remarkably short argument.

Two facts about polynomials

Lemma 1 (Interpolation) If \(E \subseteq \mathbb{F}^n\) is finite and \(\left|E\right| < \binom{d+n}{n}\), then some nonzero polynomial \(P \in \mathbb{F}[x_1, \dots, x_n]\) of degree at most \(d\) vanishes on \(E\).

Proof. The polynomials of degree at most \(d\) form a vector space of dimension

\[ \dim \mathbb{F}[x_1, \dots, x_n]_{\le d} = \binom{d+n}{n}, \tag{1}\]

since the right side counts monomials of degree at most \(d\). Evaluation at the points of \(E\) is a linear map from this space to \(\mathbb{F}^E\). By Equation 1 the domain has larger dimension than the codomain, so the kernel is nontrivial.

Lemma 2 If \(Q \in \mathbb{F}_q[x_1, \dots, x_n]\) is nonzero and has degree less than \(q\) in each variable, then \(Q\) does not vanish on all of \(\mathbb{F}_q^n\).

Proof. Induct on \(n\). For \(n = 1\), a nonzero polynomial of degree less than \(q\) has fewer than \(q\) roots. For \(n > 1\), write \(Q = \sum_{i < q} Q_i(x_1, \dots, x_{n-1})\, x_n^i\) with some \(Q_i \ne 0\). By induction there is \(a \in \mathbb{F}_q^{n-1}\) with \(Q_i(a) \ne 0\), so \(Q(a, x_n)\) is a nonzero polynomial in \(x_n\) of degree less than \(q\), and it has a non-root.

The theorem

Theorem 1 (Dvir, 2009) Every Kakeya set \(K \subseteq \mathbb{F}_q^n\) satisfies \[ \left|K\right| \ge \binom{q+n-1}{n} \ge \frac{q^n}{n!}. \]

Proof. Suppose \(\left|K\right| < \binom{q+n-1}{n}\). Applying Lemma 1 with \(d = q - 1\) gives a nonzero polynomial \(P\) of degree at most \(q - 1\) that vanishes on \(K\). Let \(d = \deg P\) and let \(P_d\) be the homogeneous part of \(P\) of degree \(d\). Since \(K\) is nonempty, \(P\) is not a nonzero constant, so \(d \ge 1\).

Fix a direction \(v \ne 0\) and choose \(x\) with \(x + tv \in K\) for all \(t \in \mathbb{F}_q\). The polynomial \(g(t) = P(x + tv)\) has degree at most \(d \le q - 1\) and vanishes at all \(q\) elements of \(\mathbb{F}_q\), so \(g \equiv 0\). Its coefficient of \(t^d\) is \(P_d(v)\), hence \(P_d(v) = 0\). Since \(P_d\) is homogeneous of degree \(d \ge 1\), also \(P_d(0) = 0\).

So \(P_d\) vanishes on all of \(\mathbb{F}_q^n\), yet it is nonzero with degree less than \(q\) in each variable. This contradicts Lemma 2.

NoteRemark

Using polynomials that vanish to high multiplicity, Dvir, Kopparty, Saraf, and Sudan improved the constant in Theorem 1 from \(1/n!\) to \(2^{-n}\).
